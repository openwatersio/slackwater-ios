# Slackwater vs Actual Currents

**Short version.** Actual Currents shows you the water moving: animated particles over a
nautical chart, with a time scrubber, a wind overlay and route analysis. It is the best
picture of flow on a phone. It streams all of it, so it needs a connection, and the free
tier is a three-hour window. Slackwater predicts each station on the phone with no
connection, leads with the slack window, and the core is free. Both cover Canada.

They are different tools and plenty of people will want both.

## What each one answers

Actual Currents answers "what is the whole strait doing at 16:30?" Drag the scrubber and
watch Rosario Strait turn. If you are planning a route across a body of water and want to
see the set along the way, nothing else on iPhone does this.

Slackwater answers "can I get through this pass, and when?" Pick the station, and the
readout is the state now, the next slack and how long it lasts, max flood and ebb with
speed and set, over a curve you scrub through the week, with the reference port's tide on
the same screen. It also does tides, which Actual Currents does not.

## Offline

Actual Currents streams its model; there is no offline mode. Slackwater computes on the
phone. In the pass itself, with no bars, one of them works.

## Free tier

Actual Currents' free tier is three hours either side of now. Planning tomorrow's departure
is $4.99 a month or $39.99 a year. Slackwater's predictions are free for any date.

## What Actual Currents has that Slackwater does not

The flow picture, the wind overlay, route planning with current analysis, and the global
ocean surface currents. Slackwater is a station app and does not draw a flow field.

## Who should pick which

**Actual Currents** if you want to see the flow across a whole area, plan a route against
it, and you do that at the dock with signal.

**Slackwater** if you want the slack window at a station, offline, free, with tides.

**Both** if you plan on Actual Currents and run on Slackwater.

Predictions are not observations. Weather, river flow and local conditions change the
water. Neither app is for navigation.
