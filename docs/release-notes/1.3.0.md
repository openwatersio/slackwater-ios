1.3.0 (27)

Tides go worldwide. 1,429 stations became 2,765, across about a hundred countries —
the Solent, the Wadden Sea, Tokyo Bay, Sydney, the Baltic. Everything still works
with no signal. The map under those stations has not caught up yet: outside the US
and Canada you get the station, the curve and the times, on bare land.

· Currents stay North American. Where there is no current station within about 20 km,
  Near Me now says so instead of leaving you to wonder — and that includes gaps inside
  home water, like Desolation Sound. Tides are unaffected.
· Near Me ranks from where you are. It used to fall back to Victoria BC, so a first
  run anywhere else opened on stations a continent away. Now it uses your fix, then
  the last station you opened, and only reaches for Victoria if it has neither. The
  map's opening view follows the same order, and the full list is plain alphabetical —
  Friday Harbor no longer sits pinned at the top.
· Duplicate harbours are gone. Lerwick arrived three times from three UK publishers;
  123 duplicates collapsed in all. One of them, Port Stanley, had two feeds
  disagreeing by 106 km, which put a Falklands pin in open ocean.
· Region lines outside North America read properly — "Portsmouth · England",
  "Fishguard · Wales", not everything reduced to the country. 1,246 of them improved.
· Ten US stations were dropped, seven of them Alaskan, and Upper Cook Inlet loses five
  of its six gauges. They advertise a tidal datum that disagrees with their own
  predictions by more than a third of a metre, which is the same check that keeps bad
  world stations out. Southampton and Penarth fail it too and are not shipped. No pin
  beats a wrong one.

Worth a look: open somewhere you know well outside North America and check the high
water against the local tide table — the times overseas are fitted from published
predictions and nothing has verified them to the minute yet, so a station that reads
an hour out is exactly the report worth having. And if you sail Cook Inlet or upper
Puget Sound, tell me what went missing.
